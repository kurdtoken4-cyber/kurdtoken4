# KURDESTAN Card 14 — Cultural Atlas Deep Audit Report — V5.18

## Audit scope
Card 14 is treated as a university-style living-heritage, intangible-cultural-heritage and lifeways atlas. This revision expands the prior V5.17 framework with deeper social-science, ethnographic, digital-heritage, safeguarding, visual-ethnography and evaluation modules.

## Included
- 64 structured thematic domains / IDs IH-001–IH-064.
- Five-language core interface labels and descriptions.
- A/B/C/D evidence model.
- Community participation, free/prior/informed consent, privacy, attribution, withdrawal and correction rules.
- Sacred/restricted knowledge handling and sensitive-location protection.
- Four-region comparison without forced cultural uniformity.
- Advanced modules covering power, gender/labour, class, minority communities, intercommunity exchange, built environment, soundscape, sensory memory, calendars, innovation, born-digital heritage, emergencies, climate, tourism and safeguarding indicators.
- Digital archive metadata standard and structured atlas record schema.
- Visual ethnography protocol and a six-image documentary register with source/licence checks.
- Explicit research-gap register.

## Academic update
The 2024 UNESCO Basic Texts/Operational Directives are treated as the current baseline. Card 14 now reflects community involvement, customary access, safeguarding, education, documentation, ethical awareness-raising, and safeguards against decontextualization, misappropriation, over-commercialisation and unsustainable tourism.

## Visual documentation
Six documentary images remain in the visual atlas. Each image has a caption, source navigation and a rights/licence review requirement. Images illustrate a context and are never treated as proof of universal or exclusive cultural ownership.

## Boundary with other cards
Card 5 = comprehensive history; Card 6 = historical/tourism/religious places; Card 7 = notable people; Card 8 = music/artists; Card 9 = handicrafts; Card 10 = clothing; Card 11 = foodways; Card 12 = language; Card 13 = research/source methodology; Card 15 = society/community. Card 14 owns living heritage, social practices, local knowledge, transmission, ritual/social context and everyday lifeways.

## Critical rules
1. Never present every Kurdish community as culturally identical.
2. Never infer exclusive ownership from a photograph, costume, food, dance or place name.
3. Record locality, community, period and evidence scope for each claim.
4. Treat community members as knowledge holders and participants, not merely subjects.
5. Obtain appropriate consent before recording or publishing identifiable people or restricted knowledge.
6. Respect customary restrictions even where public access is technically possible.
7. Record contested interpretations instead of silently choosing one.
8. Separate documented facts, scholarly interpretation and future research questions.
9. Keep rights/licence metadata attached to every media asset.
10. Re-review entries when new evidence or community corrections become available.

## V5.19 dance-focused deep audit
The Kurdish dance section was independently re-audited and expanded. It now contains 24 dedicated research domains (D01–D24) covering terminology, formations, dance leadership, gestures, rhythm, davul/zurna accompaniment, weddings, Newroz, gender and access to public initiative, youth learning, improvisation, four-region variation, Khorasan, migration memory, diaspora, staged folklore, public gatherings, costume/social signalling, performance space, transmission and audiovisual documentation.

### Evidence discipline
- Govend/halay/hełperke/dîlan are treated as overlapping but not automatically identical labels.
- No ancient origin is asserted from visual resemblance alone.
- Regional claims are locality-scoped.
- Gender patterns are documented as social variation, not universal rules.
- Staged folklore is separated from participatory living practice.
- Dance images illustrate context and are not proof of exclusive cultural ownership.
