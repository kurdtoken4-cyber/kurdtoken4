# KURDESTAN — Language & Varieties Research Notes

## Scope
This section distinguishes the three major Kurdish groupings commonly used in linguistic literature—Northern Kurdish (Kurmanji), Central Kurdish (Sorani), and Southern Kurdish—from closely related Northwestern Iranian languages/varieties such as Zazaki and Gorani/Hawrami where classifications vary.

## Evidence base
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Kermanshah vii. Languages and Dialects” (Habib Borjian): discusses Central Kurdish/Sorani, Southern Kurdish, Gurani and local variation in western Iran.
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Gurani” (David N. MacKenzie): describes Gurani as a group of Northwestern Iranian dialects and Hawrami as a major member.
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Avromani” (David N. MacKenzie): describes Hawrami as an archaic form within the Gorani group and discusses its linguistic features and contact with Kurdish.
- Wikimedia Commons, “Branches of the Kurdish language, Northern, Central, Southern.png”: CC0 map illustrating the three major Kurdish branches.
- Wikimedia Commons, “Kurdish languages map.svg”: CC BY-SA 3.0 approximate distribution map; the Commons description itself warns that boundaries and classifications are approximate.

## Editorial rule
The site does not present a disputed classification as absolute fact. Terms such as “language”, “variety” and “dialect” can depend on linguistic, historical and sociolinguistic criteria. Population figures are intentionally omitted unless a source provides a defensible, scoped estimate.
